Research

Undergraduate Thesis · Aug 2025 -- Sep 2026

Quad Sector Microstructured LSPR-Based PCF Sensor with Enhanced DPSS

Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Mohammad Rakibul Islam, Department of EEE, IUT

Designed and numerically evaluated a quad-sector microstructured photonic crystal fiber (PCF) refractive-index sensor with two spatially separated plasmonic sensing interfaces: ITO at the upper and lower sectors, and an Ag–TiO2 arrangement at the lateral sectors. The different optical responses produce two resonance branches that can be assessed through conventional wavelength sensitivity and Dual-Peak Shift Sensitivity (DPSS).

  • Developed a two-dimensional transverse eigenmode model in COMSOL Multiphysics using the finite element method (FEM), including wavelength-dependent optical properties for fused silica, ITO, silver, and titanium dioxide.
  • Examined core-guided and surface plasmon polariton (SPP) modes, electric-field distributions, phase matching, and confinement-loss spectra.
  • Conducted sequential parameter studies of ITO, Ag, and TiO2 coating thicknesses, followed by checks of air-hole dimensions and pitch, over an analyte refractive-index range of 1.33–1.38.

The final quantitative analysis is based primarily on the x-polarized response. The maximum wavelength sensitivity and DPSS were both obtained over the RI interval 1.37–1.38. Wavelength sensitivity tracks the long-wavelength resonance, while DPSS measures the change in separation between the two resonance peaks.

Scope and limitation: These are numerical simulation results, not experimental measurements. No physical sensor was fabricated or tested. The model uses bulk analyte refractive index rather than analyte-specific binding chemistry, so it does not establish selective detection of a particular biomolecule. The parameter search was discrete and sequential, not a global optimization. Experimental validation, fabrication-tolerance analysis, and the practical continuity of the nominal 1 nm Ag layer remain important next steps.
